## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

- **SLO burn-rate alerting** (seen today across Checkout, Cart, Ensemble, Frontend, order-service, cardiology-api, oncology-api): large orgs standardize a single SLO template (fast + slow burn-rate pair) applied per service via CI/CD-managed Terraform/Jsonnet, owned by each service team rather than a central SRE group, with a platform team maintaining the template library.
- **Golden signals per service** (inventory-service, order-processor): enterprises deploy this as a paved-road pattern — every new service inherits latency/traffic/error/saturation rules automatically from a service-catalog scaffold, so coverage isn't dependent on an individual engineer remembering to add alerts.
- **Multi-cloud alerting** (GCP Cloud SQL/Compute/Pub-Sub rules alongside app-level SLOs): federated Prometheus/Cortex/Mimir setups aggregate per-region, per-cluster metrics into a single pane, with recording rules pre-aggregating high-cardinality series to control cost before they hit long-term storage.
- **Runbook/skill lifecycle**: mature orgs version runbooks like code — peer-reviewed, linked from alert annotations, and retired when a service is decommissioned — rather than as static wiki pages that drift out of date.
- **Team structure**: platform engineering owns alerting infrastructure and SLO tooling; AppDev/service teams own thresholds and runbook content; SRE/on-call owns escalation and postmortems — with clear handoff via labels on alert rules (team, service, severity).
- **Volume considerations**: at scale, teams cap cardinality via recording rules and drop rules on raw exporters, apply log retention tiers (hot 7-14 days, cold 30-90 days) for compliance needs, and use tail-based trace sampling to keep 100% of error/slow traces while sampling healthy traffic.
