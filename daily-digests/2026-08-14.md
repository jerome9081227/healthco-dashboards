## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

- **Architecture patterns**: Per-service golden-signal rule groups (latency/error/traffic/saturation) plus a parallel Grafana SLO-app rule group per service, both wired to the same underlying span-metrics/Prometheus data — this mirrors how larger orgs split "is the service healthy" (SRE-facing) from "are we inside our customer-facing objective" (product/SLO-facing) without duplicating instrumentation.
- **Operational workflows**: `health=error` triage is typically owned by the platform/observability team (query and pipeline correctness), while `firing` with clean health is routed to the owning service team via notification policies and labels (e.g., `team:aci`, `service_name:...`) — separating "the monitoring is broken" from "the service is broken" keeps on-call load honest.
- **Team structures**: A platform/SRE group typically owns the shared rule groups, dashboards-as-code, and cost-guardrail alerts (Anthropic/OpenAI/GCP); individual AppDev teams own their service's golden-signal thresholds and runbook content once handed off.
- **Volume considerations**: With 200+ active rules and Adaptive Metrics usage-alerting already in place (per-pod series-count thresholds), cardinality control is being actively managed rather than left to grow — this is the pattern larger tenants need once host/service counts scale past a few hundred.

- **MTTR**: Splitting "broken query" (health=error) from "real breach" (clean firing) shortens the first-response decision — teams stop spending initial minutes disproving a false alarm before addressing the actual issue.

- **Reliability improvements**: Every core service (gateway, payments, ledger, fraud, storefront, accounts, order/inventory/shipping) already has an SLO with burn-rate paging — error-budget consumption is visible before a hard SLA breach, not after.
