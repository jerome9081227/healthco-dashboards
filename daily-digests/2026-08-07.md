## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

General patterns (illustrative, not specific to a named customer), tied to what exists in this environment:

- **Golden-signal + SLO burn-rate alerting**: large orgs generate one alert-rule template (error rate, latency p95/p99, traffic, saturation) per service via CI/CD or Terraform, then layer multi-window SLO burn-rate alerts (5m→3d) on top so paging maps to error-budget consumption instead of raw metric noise — mirrored here by the `aci-accounts`/`aci-fraud`/`aci-ledger`/`aci-loadgen` "Availability (Grafana SLO) rules" groups.
- **Federated ownership**: platform engineering owns the rule template and recording-rule library; individual service teams own their own thresholds and on-call rotation inside that template — the per-service `aci-*` grouping visible here is a direct analog.
- **Alert correlation across a service group**: when multiple golden-signal rules for related services (gateway, payments, loadgen) fire together, mature SRE orgs treat that as a single correlated incident rather than three separate pages — typically enforced via alert grouping/inhibition rules in the notification policy, not left to on-call to notice manually.
- **Runbook-as-skill**: encoding the triage checklist (validate → scope → correlate → mitigate → close) as a reusable, queryable skill — as already done here — lets any on-call engineer or the assistant itself follow a consistent first response instead of ad hoc exploration.
- **Cardinality/retention discipline**: at scale, teams cap high-cardinality labels out of alerting rules and rely on recording rules for expensive aggregations (as seen in the `grafana_slo_*` families here) to keep evaluation cost and noise manageable as service count grows.
