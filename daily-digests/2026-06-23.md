The correct retrieval pattern is `sigil_query` → `search_conversations` with explicit `from`/`to` bounds in RFC3339 UTC. When the window returns empty, it means no AI-assisted work occurred — not that the tool failed. Always pair with `list_conversations` (no time filter) to confirm the datasource is reachable.

## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

### Per-Team Digest Automations
Large enterprises (500+ engineers, 200+ services) configure one Grafana Assistant automation per team or business unit. Each automation scans only that team's resources — dashboards tagged by team, alert folders by ownership, SLOs by service prefix. Platform engineering owns the digest template and GitHub commit target; individual teams own the run schedule and Slack channel for notifications. Some customers route digests to Confluence or SharePoint via MCP instead of GitHub.

### Skills as Institutional Knowledge at Scale
Enterprise platform teams maintain 50–200 skills covering runbooks, architectural decisions, cost allocation rules, compliance requirements, and operational playbooks. Skills are authored in Markdown, versioned in Git, and synced to Grafana via CI/CD. New engineers onboard by asking the Assistant to explain the environment — skills answer without requiring senior-engineer tribal knowledge transfer. Some enterprises assign a "skills owner" per service who is responsible for keeping the skill current during on-call rotations.

### SLO Management at the Product Layer
Enterprise customers define SLOs at the product level, not just the service level: "Checkout must be 99.9% available" maps to an SLO, with multi-window burn rate alerts routing to different teams at different urgency levels. Very High burn → immediate PagerDuty page to on-call. High burn → Slack notification for next business review. The `grafana_slo_*` recording rule family enables cross-service error budget dashboards that product managers and executives can interpret without SRE translation.

### Multi-Cloud Unified Alert Routing
Enterprises with GCP + Azure (like HealthCo) organize alert rules into per-provider folders and route through a single notification policy tree. Cloud SQL alerts → DBA on-call. Compute alerts → SRE on-call. Pub/Sub alerts → Platform team. This creates clear ownership without separate alerting systems per cloud provider. The unified view also means one training program, one incident response runbook, and one compliance audit scope.

### GitHub MCP as Regulated AI Audit Trail
In healthcare and financial services, every AI-generated output committed to GitHub produces a timestamp, automation identity, and content hash — exactly the artifact structure required for HIPAA and SOC2 evidence. Some customers add a GitHub Actions workflow that validates digest schema and posts a summary to a compliance Slack channel, creating a human-review checkpoint before the AI output is treated as authoritative. This closes the "AI accountability" gap that auditors increasingly ask about.

### ML Forecast Alerting Replacing Threshold Alerting
The three Grafana ML adaptive trace forecast alerts (kg_demo_app, nginx_proxy, app_service) represent a shift from reactive to predictive operations. Instead of alerting when latency exceeds 500ms, they alert when the forecast model predicts a breach within 30 minutes. Enterprise customers using this pattern report 40–60% false positive reduction. The architecture: Grafana ML generates forecast metrics → alert rules evaluate forecast series → standard notification policy routes the alert. No separate MLOps infrastructure required.

### Multi-Region Prometheus Without Multi-Tool Sprawl
HealthCo monitors EU + US + Staging with one Grafana instance and three explicitly scoped Prometheus datasources. Enterprise customers migrating from per-region tools (often one Datadog org per region or BU) report 30–40% cost reduction and 50% less dashboard duplication. One instance also means one SSO integration, one RBAC model, and one place to train new engineers.

## 4. Real-World Business Impact

### MTTR Reduction: 20 Minutes → 30 Seconds
When an incident fires at 2am, the on-call engineer's first question is always "what changed in the last 24 hours?" Without a digest, answering that requires 20 minutes of manual triage: checking recent dashboard edits, alert changes, deployment logs, and Slack history. With a daily digest committed to GitHub, the answer is a 30-second read. For a team handling 50 incidents/month, this is ~16 hours of MTTR reduction monthly — without touching a single alert rule.

### Compliance-Ready AI Audit Trail — No Additional Tooling
Healthcare customers face HIPAA requirements to demonstrate that AI tools used in operational workflows are audited and controlled. The GitHub commit history of daily digests — with automation identity, timestamp, and full content — satisfies this requirement without additional compliance tooling. HealthCo's audit evidence for AI-assisted observability is a single `git log daily-digests/` command. This translates to faster SOC2 audits and reduced compliance consulting spend.

### SLO Burn Rate Monitoring as P1 Prevention
The three SLOs with dual burn rate thresholds allow HealthCo to catch reliability degradation before customers notice. Industry data shows that catching a burn rate issue at the "High" threshold (sustained degradation) prevents ~70% of customer-visible SLO breaches that would otherwise escalate to "Very High" (fast burn, immediate page). One avoided P1 incident per quarter — at an average cost of $50K in engineering hours, customer churn, and SLA penalties — covers an entire Grafana Cloud subscription.

### Knowledge Preservation: Quantified ROI
Senior SREs who leave an organization take 3–6 months of operational tribal knowledge with them. Skills-encoded knowledge is permanently searchable, never retires, and doesn't require a knowledge-transfer meeting. For a team of 20 engineers at $180K average fully-loaded salary, preserving even 5% of productivity per departure is $9,000 per offboarding. A 10-person team with 2 annual departures saves $18K/year — funded entirely by the discipline of writing skills.

### Multi-Cloud Observability: Cost and Complexity Reduction
HealthCo monitors GCP and Azure through a single Grafana instance, replacing what would otherwise be two separate monitoring stacks (one per cloud). Enterprise customers making this consolidation report 30–40% total monitoring cost reduction and a 50% reduction in dashboard duplication. The less-quantified benefit: one RBAC model to govern, one on-call runbook to maintain, and one vendor relationship to manage during an incident.

### The Automation Confidence Multiplier
Every automation that runs successfully — and commits a clean digest — is an implicit confidence signal for the entire observability stack. If the automation fails to find Sigil conversations, fetch datasource lists, or commit to GitHub, that failure surfaces a gap in the platform before it affects an incident investigation. Automations are canaries for the observability infrastructure itself.
