## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

**SLO-based alerting (Checkout, Cart, Ensemble, Frontend, order-service, cardiology-api, oncology-api)**
- Large customers standardize on multi-window, multi-burn-rate SLO alerts (as configured here) precisely to avoid paging on short blips while still catching fast burns — this pattern scales to hundreds of services without a linear increase in on-call noise.
- Ownership is typically federated: platform engineering owns the SLO framework and recording-rule conventions (the `grafana_slo_*` family here); each AppDev team owns the objective and error budget for its own service.

**Golden-signal alerting per service (inventory-service, order-processor)**
- At scale, golden-signal rule sets (latency, traffic, errors, saturation) are generated from a template per service rather than hand-written, so onboarding a new microservice takes minutes, not days.
- `health=error` rules are typically surfaced on a platform-wide "alerting health" dashboard so platform SRE can catch broken rules org-wide, separate from the service teams reacting to actual firing alerts.

**Multi-cloud, multi-region observability (GCP + Azure, EU/US Prod, US Staging)**
- Enterprises isolate Prometheus instances by region/environment (as done here) to bound cardinality and blast radius, then federate a subset of high-value metrics into a global view for cross-region SLO rollups.
- Infrastructure alerts (Cloud SQL, Compute Engine, Pub/Sub) are owned by a central cloud platform team; application alerts are owned by service teams — mirrors the folder split already in place (`cloud-provider---gcp` vs. service-specific folders).

**Skills/runbooks as reusable memory**
- At scale, teams maintain a small number of "environment reference" skills (datasources, naming conventions, folder structure) rather than repeating discovery every session — exactly the role the `HealthCo Grafana Environment Reference` skill plays here — reducing ramp-up time for new engineers and for AI-assisted troubleshooting.

**Automations for recurring reporting**
- Scheduled digest/report automations are commonly used by platform teams to produce daily/weekly rollups for leadership without manual effort; the pattern here (scan state → summarize → commit to a Git-backed audit trail) is a common lightweight alternative to a dedicated BI pipeline for small-to-mid observability teams.
