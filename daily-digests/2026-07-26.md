## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

- **Multi-cloud, multi-region alerting architecture**: Large customers mirror HealthCo's pattern — dedicated alert folders per cloud provider (GCP, Azure) and per environment (EU Production, US Production, US Staging) — but add a platform-owned "shared" folder for cross-cutting SLOs so service teams don't duplicate burn-rate logic per region.
- **Golden signals as a standard contract**: Enterprises standardize the four golden-signal rule set (latency, traffic, errors, saturation) as a template applied to every new service at onboarding, typically enforced via a dashboard-as-code / alert-as-code pipeline (Terraform, Grafana provisioning, or Git-based sync) rather than manual UI creation.
- **Operational ownership**: Platform engineering/SRE owns the golden-signal templates, SLO framework, and notification routing; individual AppDev teams own thresholds and runbook content for their specific services. Firing alerts like the current `inventory-service`/`order-processor` incidents route to the owning team via labels, not a central on-call queue.
- **Volume and cardinality management**: at HealthCo's current scale (50+ rules, single-digit services with golden signals), cardinality isn't yet a constraint. Enterprises with hundreds of services manage this via recording rules for burn-rate SLIs (already used here — `grafana_slo_sli_5m/30m/1h/2h/6h/1d/3d`), label allow-lists on high-cardinality exporters, and tiered log/trace retention (hot logs 7–14 days, cold/archive for compliance beyond that).
- **Runbook lifecycle**: Enterprises keep runbooks (like the "Prometheus alert investigation" skill here) versioned alongside alert rules and link them via `runbookUrl` annotations, so triage steps stay current as the alert's PromQL/thresholds change.
