## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

### AI Observability (Sigil) at Scale
Large enterprises instrument every LLM-powered product (internal copilots, customer-facing assistants, code review bots) through Grafana AI Observability. Teams configure **evaluation rules** to automatically score every conversation on dimensions like correctness, safety, and hallucination rate. Platform engineering owns the Sigil datasource; individual product teams query filtered views scoped to their agent namespace.

### Daily Digest Automation at Scale
Enterprise SRE and platform teams run scheduled Assistant Automations (via `manage_automations`) to generate nightly summaries — not just conversation digests, but also alert fatigue reports, SLO burn rate summaries, and on-call handoff notes. These are committed to Git repos (via GitHub/GitLab MCP), posted to Slack channels, and linked in incident tickets. A single automation cron covers the whole org; individual teams receive filtered output via label-scoped queries.

### GitHub MCP at Scale
Enterprises use GitHub MCP to close the loop between observability insights and code repositories:
- Runbooks are updated automatically when investigations surface new root-cause patterns.
- Dashboard-as-code (JSON/YAML) is committed and PR'd rather than applied directly, enabling review workflows and rollback.
- Daily digests become a Git-native audit trail — searchable, diffable, and PR-reviewable by compliance teams.
At scale, organizations enforce branch protection rules so automation commits go to a feature branch and require a review before merging to `main`.

### Conversation Review Workflows at Scale
Enterprises with 10k+ daily LLM interactions use Sigil's `search_conversations` with agent/namespace filters rather than `list_conversations`. They define **evaluation collections** (curated test sets) and track pass rates per agent version over time. Platform teams receive weekly digest emails generated by automations that call Sigil, aggregate scores, and format reports — no manual review required.

## 4. Real-World Business Impact

**Reduced MTTR through automated knowledge capture**
Daily digests eliminate the "lost context" problem between shifts. When an on-call engineer picks up a ticket at 6am, the overnight digest — committed to Git — tells them exactly what was investigated, what was configured, and what was learned. This compresses investigation ramp-up from 30–60 minutes to under 5 minutes.

**Compliance and audit readiness**
Regulated industries (healthcare, finance) require evidence that AI-assisted decisions were reviewed and logged. A Git-committed daily digest provides an immutable, timestamped record of AI activity — directly usable in SOC 2, HIPAA, or ISO 27001 audits without additional tooling.

**Operational knowledge accumulation**
Over weeks of daily digests, a corpus of prompt patterns, query templates, and investigation playbooks builds up in the repository. New team members onboard faster by reading historical digests rather than shadowing senior engineers. Enterprises report 20–40% faster onboarding for SRE roles when Git-native knowledge bases are maintained this way.

**Cost accountability for AI usage**
Sigil conversation data feeds directly into Grafana's Cost Management and Billing App (CMAB). Daily digests that include generation counts and model usage help FinOps teams attribute AI spend to specific teams, products, or features — enabling chargeback models and surfacing underutilized or over-provisioned AI capacity.

**Reliability improvement through pattern detection**
When digests are generated consistently, week-over-week diffs reveal patterns: which services generate the most investigations, which alert rules fire most often, which dashboards are queried daily. These patterns drive proactive reliability investments — fixing root causes before they become incidents, rather than reacting after.
