## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

### Multi-Domain SLO Folder Architecture — RBAC by Clinical Service Line
HealthCo's 4-folder SLO structure mirrors the pattern used by large healthcare enterprises:
- **Folder per domain** = a Grafana RBAC role boundary; cardiology engineers can only edit their folder, oncology theirs
- **Shared services folder** = platform engineering team ownership; they control checkout, cart, and frontend SLOs
- At 100+ SLOs across 20 clinical departments, this structure prevents accidental cross-team SLO threshold changes — a HIPAA change management control
- Each SLO auto-generates 13 recording rules + 2 alerting rules; at 100 SLOs that is 1,500 managed rules — organized folders are the only viable management structure

### Golden Signal Alerting as a Service Production Readiness Gate
Enterprise platform teams use the golden signal rule group pattern as a ship-gate:
- Every service must have all 4 golden signal rules in `state=normal` (not `health=error`) before it can be promoted to production
- A meta-alert watches for any `health=error` rule in any golden signal group for >10 minutes and pages the platform team
- This converts Grafana's rule health API into a service readiness dashboard — "is this service actually monitored?" becomes a queryable, alertable fact
- At 50+ services, manual verification is impossible; the meta-alert is the only scalable approach

### Multi-Window Burn Rate Alerting at Scale — Google SRE Pattern
The dual alert (Very High = fast-burn, High = slow-burn) is the production-hardened multi-window approach:
- Fast-burn (1h window): pages on-call immediately via PagerDuty
- Slow-burn (6h window): creates a Jira ticket and posts to Slack — no page required
- Enterprises configure different notification policies per severity; the HealthCo SLO folder must map to this routing pattern
- At 12 SLOs with dual alerts, that is 24 alert rules requiring correct notification routing; Grafana's notification policy tree handles this with label matchers, eliminating per-rule configuration

### Alert Rule Health as a Platform Engineering KPI
Tracking `health=error` rule count is a tier-1 platform engineering metric:
- **Target:** 0 rules in `health=error` for >5 minutes in production
- **Dashboard:** 4 stat panels — Total Rules, Normal, Firing, health=error
- **Alert:** "Any golden signal rule in health=error for >10 minutes" → platform on-call page
- The `health=error` count is more dangerous than the `firing` count: a firing alert means something is wrong but you know about it; a `health=error` means something might be wrong and you don't know

### Cross-Layer Alert Inventory Review as a Weekly Operational Practice
The pattern of running `alerting_manage_rules` with no filter and scanning across GCP, Kubernetes, application, and frontend layers is a weekly SRE practice at scale:
- Any rule added since last week that is in `health=error` is reviewed first
- Any SLO firing on very-high burn rate is escalated
- GCP layer serves as the baseline: if GCP is clean, the problem is in the app layer
- This cross-layer triage takes <5 minutes in Grafana Assistant and replaces a 30-minute manual review across 4 separate console tabs
