- **Architecture**: Organizations running 200+ alert rules across many services typically generate golden-signal rule sets (latency, traffic, errors, saturation) from a shared template per service, with multi-window SLO burn-rate alerting layered on top for services closest to revenue or, in healthcare, patient-facing systems (e.g. the `cardiology-api` / `oncology-api` SLOs seen here).
- **Operational workflows**: When the same four golden-signal rules go into `health=error` for multiple services at once, mature SRE practice is to route that to the platform/tooling owner as one shared-infrastructure ticket (broken template, datasource, or credential) rather than paging each service team independently — collapsing N symptomatic alerts into 1 root-cause fix.
- **Team structures**: Platform/SRE typically owns the rule-generation template, alerting pipeline, and any digest/automation tooling; service teams own their specific thresholds and business logic; a documentation owner keeps environment-reference material in sync with the live stack so automated tooling doesn't reason from stale or unverifiable facts — directly relevant given today's reproducibility gap.
- **Volume considerations**: At 200+ rules (with pagination caps already being hit), enterprises typically add rule-inventory diffing to CI, cap SLO recording-rule sprawl (each SLO here generates roughly a dozen recording rules), and avoid relying on any single tool call's page limit for compliance-relevant counts.
