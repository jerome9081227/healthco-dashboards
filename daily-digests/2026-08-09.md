## 3. How Enterprise Customers Use This at Scale

- **Dashboards**: Large healthcare/regulated customers typically standardize on templated, tag-driven dashboards per service (e.g., the `aci-*` naming convention seen here) provisioned via Git/Terraform rather than click-ops, with a platform team owning shared panels and app teams owning service-specific ones.
- **Alert Rules & SLOs**: Enterprises federate SLO definitions (availability, latency) per service and route via multi-window multi-burn-rate rules — exactly the pattern present in this tenant (5m/30m/1h/2h/6h/1d/3d windows per service). At scale this is managed centrally (SLO-as-code) so every new service inherits the same rule set automatically.
- **Skills/Runbooks**: Mature SRE orgs keep runbooks as versioned, searchable skills (as done here) so on-call responders get consistent triage steps regardless of who's paged, reducing tribal knowledge risk.
- **Automations**: Scheduled assistant automations (daily digests, weekly SLO reports) are typically owned by a platform/observability team, run at tenant scope with restricted write permissions, and their output is archived to a Git repo for auditability — mirroring this exact workflow.
- **Team structure**: Platform engineering owns golden-signal instrumentation and SLO tooling; AppDev teams own service-level thresholds and receive/escalate alerts; SRE/on-call owns cross-service incident correlation (e.g., the simultaneous aci-gateway/aci-payments firing pattern flagged above).
- **Volume considerations**: With ~13 SLO rules per service and 3 golden-signal rules per service, a healthcare platform with dozens of microservices can accumulate hundreds to low-thousands of active rules — cardinality and rule-evaluation cost management (via recording rules, as used here) becomes a first-class concern.
